NGTL Fundamentals

Transportation fundamentals for TC Energy's NGTL / Foothills system — the pipeline network that gathers and moves gas across Alberta and BC toward AECO and export

— · updated —

NGTL is the gathering and transmission system that collects gas from wells across Alberta and northeast BC and moves it toward the AECO/NIT trading hub and export points at the Alberta/BC and Empress/McNeill borders. How much of that system is actually being used on a given day — and specifically whether a constraint is eating into firm capacity or just trimming interruptible flow — is a real input into how a fundamentals desk reads Alberta basis. This page tracks that directly from TC Energy's own published capability, flow, and outage data, updated daily.

One thing this deliberately does not do: show a daily AECO spot price. Daily AECO data sits behind NGI, ICE, and Nasdaq Data Link paywalls, so the only price series here is the AER's official monthly reference price — and every correlation below is shown with its sample size, because eighteen months of history isn't enough to claim one.

Firm vs. interruptible impact

NGTL sells transportation as distinct contract services, and interruptible service is explicitly "subject to available capacity" while firm service isn't — so IT flow absorbs a constraint first, and most outages never actually threaten firm transportation. TC Energy says which case applies directly, in each outage's own "Type of Restriction" field; this reads that field rather than treating every outage as equally significant.

FT-R
Firm Transportation – Receipt, at a shipper's specific receipt point(s)
FT-D
Firm Transportation – Delivery, at Group 1/2/3 delivery locations
IT-R / IT-D
Interruptible Receipt/Delivery — priced off the firm rate, but curtailed first, "subject to available capacity"

Source: NGTL System Services Summary Matrix, TC Energy Customer Express — publicly posted, not behind the shipper login.

This also lines up with the standard NAESB nomination cycle every North American shipper works within — Timely, Evening, and Intraday 1–3 — where firm nominations get priority in every cycle. NGTL's own cycle deadlines sit behind the shipper portal rather than the public site above, so this project claims the general framework, not NGTL's exact clock times.

Why IT gets curtailed first: it costs more, not less

Interruptible service isn't a discount option — NGTL's own posted tariff prices IT above the equivalent firm rate at every point compared below. A shipper on IT is paying a premium for flexibility and accepting curtailment risk in exchange; it's not that IT is cheap capacity nobody wanted.
